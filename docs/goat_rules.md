# GOAT rules profile

Target: TCG GOAT Format with April 2005 Forbidden/Limited list and the widely used August 17, 2005 card pool. The rule profile records first-turn draw and historical Ignition priority. Full validation is pending broader native regression coverage.

Sources: [GoatFormat mechanics](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html), [card pool and banlist](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html), and [ocgcore GOAT flags](https://github.com/edo9300/ygopro-core/blob/master/ocgapi_constants.h).

The M0 native probe confirms that a pinned core configured with `DUEL_MODE_GOAT` performs an opening draw for the first player in its vanilla-monster fixture. This is one startup behavior check, not a complete historical rules validation.

The M1 deck gate uses a pinned [Project Ignis GOAT whitelist](https://github.com/ProjectIgnis/LFLists/blob/master/GOAT.lflist.conf) and checks copy limits against the pinned main and [GOAT-variant card databases](https://github.com/ProjectIgnis/BabelCDB/blob/master/goat-entries.cdb). For example, the GOAT list allows two copies of Nobleman of Crossout under variant ID `504700116`; the ordinary ID `71044499` is intentionally rejected. [GoatFormat's card-pool guide](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html) also places Nobleman at two copies, and [its EDOPro guide](https://www.goatformat.com/home/what-is-edopro) explains the custom GOAT IDs. Full membership still needs an independent comparison with the August 17, 2005 card pool.

**Deck sizes:** [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says the historical Main and Fusion Decks had no maximum size. The native adapter now accepts Main Decks with at least 40 cards and has a 61-card startup regression; larger decks have not been stress tested. The same guide says historical Side Decks were empty or exactly 15 cards. The default `RulesProfile` enforces that size rule. `RulesProfile(side_deck_policy="community")` allows 0–15 cards, reflecting the guide's note that short Side Decks are rarely disallowed in present-day online play. Sideboarding itself is not implemented.

The pinned Nobleman variant script targets a set monster and banishes it. The normal-monster regression confirms that the target stays unidentified in the opponent's legal action until it is revealed on resolution, then appears in the banished zone. This agrees with the [historical Nobleman ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) that the target is removed from play without going to the Graveyard. A second native fixture targets set Dekoichi (`87621407`), causing both players' remaining Decks to be shown for verification. [Historical tournament rulings](https://ms.yugipedia.com/a/a1/2003_tournament_rulings.pdf) require both players to show their Decks when a Flip Effect Monster is removed this way. The pinned core sends a separate `MSG_CONFIRM_CARDS` to each recipient; observations include only the addressed message, as a card multiset without native sequence numbers. The resulting duel reaches terminal state and its full schema-v10 trajectory replay-verifies. A third fixture targets set Magician of Faith with two more copies in each player's Deck and another copy in the activating player's hand. It verifies that Nobleman banishes the set target and all four Deck copies, leaves the hand copy intact, and sends no Magician of Faith to the Graveyard. This follows the same [historical card FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html), which restricts the additional removal to matching Deck copies. [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) notes that many online players skip deck verification to avoid persistent screenshots; the tested engine branch follows the reveal-and-verify ruling. Other Nobleman interactions remain unaudited.

**Book of Moon into Nobleman:** [Historical Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) allow it to turn a face-up monster face-down. [Nobleman's historical card FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) requires a face-down target and removes a non-Flip monster without a matching-Deck search. A seeded native regression has the opponent Normal Summon Breaker the Magical Warrior, then uses Book of Moon on Breaker and Nobleman on the resulting hidden field slot in the next Main Phase. Breaker is banished without entering the Graveyard, and no private Deck reveal occurs. This covers a common two-card removal line; chained responses and Breaker's own counter effect during this line remain untested.

**Ignition priority after a summon:** [GoatFormat's basic mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) states that after a Summon resolves, the turn player may activate a monster's Ignition Effect before the opponent may respond with a fast effect; its Cannon Soldier example uses this timing. The native regression test sets Book of Moon on the opponent's field, then Summons Cannon Soldier. After the summon-negation window, the core offers Cannon Soldier's Ignition Effect to the turn player. If that player activates it and tributes Cannon Soldier as cost, the opponent cannot use Book to target the absent monster; 500 damage resolves. If the turn player passes, the opponent receives a Book of Moon chain option and can turn Cannon Soldier facedown before its Ignition Effect is used. This tests one timing sequence, not the full priority system.

**Breaker summon trigger against Book of Moon:** [Historical Breaker rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) say its counter placement starts a chain after Normal Summon; the counter is unavailable until that chain resolves. The same rulings say Breaker receives no counter when Flip Summoned. [GoatFormat's priority guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) uses Breaker as the example where an opponent can respond to the placement trigger before Breaker's Ignition Effect becomes available. A seeded native fixture has the opponent chain a previously Set Book of Moon to that trigger. Breaker is face-down after the chain, with no Spell Counter and no immediately available destruction effect. When Flip Summoned on its controller's next turn, it remains at 1600 ATK with no counter or destruction effect. The fixture distinguishes this window from chaining Book after Breaker has already gained and spent its counter; other responses to the trigger remain unaudited.

**Field Spell replacement:** [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says only one face-up Field Spell can remain active; when a new Field Spell activation resolves, the previous one is destroyed. One native test activates pre-errata Necrovalley (`511002998`) for player 0, then A Legendary Ocean (`295517`) for player 1. At the next turn, only Ocean remains in the Field Zone and Necrovalley is in player 0's Graveyard. A second test sets Mystical Space Typhoon, then chains it to Ocean's activation and destroys Ocean before resolution; Necrovalley remains face-up. Other field interactions remain untested.

**Flip Effect:** The [official Dekoichi card database entry](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6201&ope=2&request_locale=en) states that its Flip Effect draws one card, plus one per face-up Bokoichi controlled. A native regression sets Dekoichi (`87621407`), Flip Summons it on the next turn, and verifies one card moves from deck to hand with no Bokoichi present. A second native fixture draws with Dekoichi, uses Book of Moon to turn it face-down, verifies the opponent cannot see its identity in the field observation and it cannot be Flip Summoned again that turn, then Flip Summons it on a later turn for a second effect draw. [GoatFormat's Flip Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/flip-control.html) lists Dekoichi and Book of Moon together, and the [historical Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) describe turning Flip monsters face-down. The [GoatFormat Dekoichi rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) add card-specific cases involving Bokoichi at resolution; those cases are not yet covered.

**Cyber Jar:** [GoatFormat's Cyber Jar rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) require both players to reveal their five cards before either starts placing monsters; the turn player places theirs first. A native regression with GOAT variant `504700188` verifies both public Deck-top reveals are available at the first placement decision, followed by five turn-player and five opponent placement choices. It confirms that an opponent monster placed face-down has no identity in the acting player's field observation. The reveal records retain the publicly shown card multiset; they do not identify a shuffled face-down field slot. Other Cyber Jar branches and card interactions remain unaudited.

**Ground Collapse:** [The official Ground Collapse card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5170&ope=2) says its controller chooses two unoccupied Monster Zones and neither player may use them. A pinned native fixture activates `90502999`, chooses its own Monster Zones 0 and 1 in a single six-byte response, and confirms the next Normal Summon can be placed only in Zones 2, 3, or 4. The public disabled-zone message populates both the agent observation and replay viewer. Removing Ground Collapse and interactions with other zone restrictions still need native regressions.

**Question:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5494&ope=2&request_locale=ae) forbids the guessing player from checking the activating player's Graveyard when naming its bottom monster. A seeded native fixture reaches the `MSG_ANNOUNCE_CARD` prompt: the adapter offers Monster names from the pinned GOAT whitelist, hides identities in the activating player's Graveyard for that decision, and a correct announcement banishes the bottom monster. The catalog includes Forbidden cards because a name announcement is not a Deck inclusion. It expands GOAT-variant aliases to their original printed-name IDs, then excludes alias records and Tokens rejected by the pinned core's declaration expression. In a second seeded fixture, naming Harpie Lady by its original ID (`76812113`) correctly banishes the GOAT-variant card (`54415063`); the core's `GetCode()` resolves that variant to its alias. The catalog has not been independently checked against every card name available by August 2005, so complete historical name legality remains unverified. [Historical Question rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) also cover Special Summon restrictions that remain untested.

**Exchange:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5218&ope=2&request_locale=en) says both players reveal their hands and each adds one card from the other's hand. A seeded native fixture activates `5556668`; the core sends one hand reveal to each recipient before their selection. Each action exposes only card names in that recipient's reveal, without the opponent hand's native slot index. Both selected cards move to the new controller's hand. The other player's observation receives only their own addressed reveal. [Historical Exchange rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) also forbid activation when Exchange is its controller's only hand card; that branch remains untested.

**Fruits of Kozaky's Studies:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6220&ope=2&request_locale=en) lets its controller look at the top three Deck cards and return them in any order. A native fixture offers all six `MSG_SORT_CARD` permutations, verifies that a reversed choice changes the Deck order, and confirms no Deck-top reveal reaches the opponent. This covers one sorting effect; further card-specific work will prioritize frequently played GOAT cards.

**Damage Step activation:** [GoatFormat's historical mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) distinguishes GOAT Damage Step activation windows from current rules. A paired native regression attacks a set Dekoichi while its controller has a set Book of Moon or Compulsory Evacuation Device. The core offers each card in the Battle Step attack response window. Once Dekoichi flips face-up during the Damage Step, that card remains set but is no longer a legal chain choice; Dekoichi's Flip Effect subsequently draws one card. The [historical Compulsory rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) explicitly exclude its Damage Step activation. These fixtures cover two activation restrictions and one Flip Effect timing sequence, not every Damage Step substep. The adapter labels `battle_step` and `damage_step` from public core events. It does not expose the six historical Damage Step substeps individually.

**Missed timing:** The [historical Peten the Dark Clown rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) say its optional “when… you can” trigger cannot activate when Cannon Soldier tributes it as a cost, because Cannon Soldier's effect resolves afterward. A native regression summons Cannon Soldier and Peten, tributes Peten for 500 damage, and verifies that no Peten trigger is offered while two other copies remain in the Deck. A positive control destroys a set Peten in battle, accepts its trigger, banishes it as cost, and Special Summons another copy. These cases validate this specific optional trigger; broader effects triggered mid-chain remain open.

**Trigger ordering:** [GoatFormat's historical SEGOC example](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says that when Sangan is tributed to Summon Thestalos, Sangan's mandatory trigger is chain link 1 and Thestalos's mandatory trigger is chain link 2. A native fixture performs that Tribute Summon and verifies both visible chain links. Thestalos resolves first, discarding from the opponent's hand, followed by Sangan adding a monster from the Deck to its controller's hand. This covers one two-trigger sequence. The Soul Exchange variant and triggers recognized mid-chain remain untested.

**Summon negation:** [GoatFormat's Solemn Judgment rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) distinguish negating a Summon from negating a Special Summon produced by another card effect. The [official Solemn Judgment card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=ae) says its activation pays half the controller's Life Points and negates a Summon. A pinned native regression sets Solemn Judgment, attempts a Normal Summon of Cannon Soldier, and chains Solemn in the summon-negation window. Player 1 goes from 8000 to 4000 LP; Cannon Soldier is destroyed and sent to player 0's Graveyard without reaching the field, no Ignition Effect is available, and the Normal Summon opportunity is spent. This tests one Normal Summon branch, not effect-based Special Summons or summon-negation triggers.

**Pre-errata Dark Magician of Chaos:** [GoatFormat's historical card text and rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) say its optional effect can add a Spell from the Graveyard when Normal Summoned, and that it is banished when it leaves the field face-up but goes to the Graveyard if it leaves face-down. A pinned native regression first resolves Pot of Greed, Summons two normal monsters over two turns, then Tributes both to Normal Summon pre-errata Dark Magician of Chaos (`511001039`). The player accepts its effect, targets Pot in the Graveyard, and returns Pot to hand. Two more native cases use Cannon Soldier to tribute it from a face-up or Book of Moon face-down position and verify the respective destination before Cannon Soldier resolves. The tests confirm the pre-errata script was loaded. Special Summons, missed timing, and other ways of leaving the field remain untested.

**Scapegoat:** [GoatFormat's historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) say that a player cannot activate Scapegoat after Summoning that turn, cannot Summon afterward, may still Set a monster, and may Tribute a Sheep Token for purposes other than a Tribute Summon. They also require four vacant Monster Card Zones and distinguish negating Scapegoat's activation, which restores Summon permission, from negating only its effect. Pinned native regressions activate the GOAT variant (`504700123`) before Summoning, choose four token zones, and verify four face-up Defense Position tokens, no available Summon, and an available monster Set. The public native player hint appears in both players' observations as `player0:cannot_summon_this_turn:scapegoat` while active and disappears at turn end. A separate branch Normal Summons first and verifies Scapegoat is unavailable that turn. Another branch reaches a later Main Phase with two monsters already controlled: Scapegoat can be Set but cannot be activated despite Normal Summons still being legal. A token branch keeps the four tokens until the next turn, verifies that they do not make a two-Tribute Dark Magician of Chaos Normal Summon legal, then Tributes one for Cannon Soldier's effect: it disappears without entering a Graveyard or banished zone, and Cannon Soldier deals 500 damage. A Solemn Judgment branch negates Scapegoat's activation: the hint appears during the chain, then disappears; no Tokens appear and a Normal Summon is again legal that turn. Negating only the effect and other token interactions remain untested.

**Goat Control Metamorphosis line:** [GoatFormat's Goat Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/goat-control.html) identifies Scapegoat, Metamorphosis, Thousand-Eyes Restrict, and Tsukuyomi as a central interaction. [Metamorphosis's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5539&ope=2) Tributes a monster to Special Summon a Fusion Monster of the same Level. A pinned fixture activates Scapegoat, confirms that Metamorphosis cannot be activated after its tokens appear that turn, then on a later turn Tributes one Sheep Token to Special Summon GOAT-variant Thousand-Eyes Restrict (`504700102`) from the Extra Deck. The token does not enter a Graveyard. Restrict equips the opponent's face-up monster, consistent with its [historical targeting and one-monster-at-a-time rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html). [Tsukuyomi's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5780&ope=2&request_locale=en) turns a face-up monster face-down; the fixture Normal Summons it, turns Restrict face-down, confirms the equipped monster reaches its owner's Graveyard, and checks that the opponent sees the set Restrict without its identity. The fixture now continues through Tsukuyomi's End Phase return to hand, an opposing second Normal Summon, and Restrict's next-turn Flip Summon and re-equip of that second monster. A second pinned fixture follows the [historical Restrict/Ring response example](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html): the defender Sets pre-errata Ring of Destruction (`511000824`), Restrict selects the defender's monster, and Ring chains to destroy that target. Both players take damage equal to its ATK; Restrict remains face-up without an equipped monster. The attacker cannot see the Ring identity while it is Set. Further repeated loops, other Fusion targets, and interruption branches remain untested.

The same pinned Restrict/Ring fixture also selects Restrict itself as Ring's target. Ring destroys Restrict before its effect resolves, the defender's monster stays on the field, and both players take damage equal to Restrict's current 0 ATK. This is the alternative response described in the [historical example](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html); later equip interactions remain unaudited.

**Graceful Charity and Delinquent Duo:** [Graceful Charity's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4812&ope=2&request_locale=en) draws three cards, then discards two. [Delinquent Duo's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4901&ope=2&request_locale=en) pays 1000 LP, makes the opponent discard a random card, then lets that opponent choose one of their remaining cards to discard. A pinned native fixture activates both in one duel. It verifies all 28 two-card choices from Graceful's eight-card hand, the three-card draw and two-card discard, Duo's LP payment and random first discard, and four choices offered only to Duo's opponent for their second discard. Both observers see only the opponent's hand count throughout. This covers the ordinary full-hand branch; interactions with replacement effects and hands too small to resolve remain untested.

**Pot of Greed, Mystical Space Typhoon, and Solemn Judgment:** The [2003 tournament rulings](https://ms.yugipedia.com/a/a1/2003_tournament_rulings.pdf) say Mystical Space Typhoon destroys a card but does not negate an effect by chaining to it. A seeded native fixture Sets Typhoon (`5318639`), activates Pot of Greed (`55144522`) on the opposing next turn, chains Typhoon to target the active Pot, and verifies that Pot still draws two cards after both Spells reach their respective Graveyards. The Pot player's hand gains one net card and their Deck loses two. In a second seeded branch, the opponent chains Solemn Judgment instead. The [official Solemn text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) specifies negating a Spell activation for half the controller's LP. The native branch pays 4000 LP, sends Pot to its owner's Graveyard, and leaves the Pot player's Deck count unchanged, with no draw. This contrasts with the separate Equip Spell case where destroying Premature Burial before resolution prevents its revival. Draw replacement and Deck-out branches remain untested.

**Reinforcement of the Army:** [Historical card rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) require showing the selected Warrior to the opponent and allow activation with no eligible Warrior left in the Deck, in which case the Deck is shown for verification and then shuffled. A seeded fixture activates GOAT-variant Reinforcement (`504700055`) in the sourced Warrior list. Its search offers the remaining Level 4 or lower Warriors by identity without native Deck slot numbers; a paired deck-order change leaves the acting player's observation and legal actions identical while changing raw core slot numbers. Choosing Mystic Swordsman LV2 reduces the Deck count by one, adds it to the searcher's hand, and sends a one-card reveal only to the opponent. A separate no-Warrior fixture activates Reinforcement successfully, adds no card, leaves the Deck count unchanged, and sends the full Deck reveal only to the opponent. Neither fixture proves every Warrior eligibility or interruption case.

**Mystic Swordsman LV2 versus a set Flip monster:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) say that when Mystic Swordsman LV2 attacks a face-down Defense Position monster, the monster is destroyed by its effect before battle, so it cannot level up from a battle destruction. A pinned fixture Normal Summons the GOAT variant (`504700077`), attacks an opposing set Dekoichi, and offers only the hidden opposing field slot as an attack target. Dekoichi goes to the Graveyard without drawing a card; neither player takes battle damage. The duel passes the End Phase without an LV2 level-up choice. A face-up target, position-changing Battle Step response, and available Mystic Swordsman LV4 remain untested.

**Blade Knight:** Its [official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5488&ope=2&request_locale=en) raises its ATK by 400 when its controller has at most one card in hand and negates effects of Flip monsters it destroys by battle while it is the only monster its controller controls. [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) say another face-down friendly monster also disables the negation. A pinned native fixture Normal Summons Blade Knight (`39507162`), Sets four Spells/Traps, and confirms that both observers see its current ATK change from 1600 to 2000 when the hand reaches one card, while the opponent sees only unidentified set backrow. Paired battle fixtures attack a set Dekoichi: when Blade Knight is alone, Dekoichi reaches the Graveyard without drawing; when a second friendly monster is present, Dekoichi draws one card. The attack target is a hidden field slot in both cases. The face-down-friendly-monster condition and other Flip monster effects remain untested.

**Don Zaloog:** Its [official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5426&ope=2&request_locale=en) offers a random opponent-hand discard or two-card Deck mill after it inflicts battle damage; [historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) say the effect is optional and also triggers when Don is attacked and inflicts the damage. A seeded native fixture Normal Summons Don (`76922029`) and makes a direct attack for 1400 damage with an opponent hand and Deck both nonempty. The acting player can decline or choose either mode using two action options that contain no opponent hand identity. Declining leaves hand and Deck unchanged; discarding reduces the opponent hand by one and publicly identifies the discarded card in the Graveyard; milling reduces the Deck by two and places both cards in the public Graveyard. A second fixture has the opponent attack Don with 800-ATK Mystical Elf. The opponent takes 600 battle damage and loses a random hand card; the Don controller receives the effect decision during the opponent's Damage Step, Don survives, and Elf goes to the Graveyard. A third native fixture has the opponent Summon Elf and Set all five other hand cards before Don attacks. With no opponent hand card to discard, the pinned script offers only the two-card Deck mill after Don's optional trigger is accepted; the Deck count drops by two, and no extra discard occurs. This is evidence for the engine's legal-choice mask in that state. An independent historical source for this exact empty-hand option limit is still needed. Damage prevention and short-Deck branches remain untested.

A paired Don decision fixture replaces one card in the opponent's privately held opening hand and reaches the same effect-choice prompt with the same seed. The two private hands differ, while the acting player's complete observation and both legal mode actions compare equal. This tests one hidden-hand differential at the decision boundary; it does not cover every way an opponent can acquire or reveal hand cards.

**Snatch Steal, Heavy Storm, and Book of Moon:** [Snatch Steal's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4898&ope=2&request_locale=en) takes control of the equipped opponent monster and gives that opponent 1000 LP during their Standby Phase. [Heavy Storm's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4891&ope=2&request_locale=en) destroys all Spells and Traps on the field. [Historical Snatch Steal rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) say the monster returns to its previous controller when Snatch Steal leaves the field, except that turning the monster face-down sends Snatch Steal to the Graveyard and leaves the monster with Snatch Steal's controller. One seeded native fixture steals an opponent monster, verifies the opponent's 1000 LP recovery, and destroys Snatch Steal with Heavy Storm; the monster returns to its original controller. A second fixture activates [Book of Moon](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5432&ope=2&request_locale=en) on the stolen monster: Snatch Steal goes to the Graveyard, the face-down monster remains under the stealing player's control, and its original controller sees an unidentified face-down field card with the previously public card identity retained in field memory. Other control-changing effects and chained interruptions remain untested.

**Heavy Storm and chained Scapegoat:** A [GOAT tournament account](https://www.goatformat.com/home/round-five-rhymus-lizo-vs-theeresak-poonsombat) records Scapegoat chained to Heavy Storm. A seeded native fixture Sets Scapegoat, has the opponent activate Heavy Storm, then chains Scapegoat. Four face-up Defense Position Sheep Tokens remain after both Spells reach their owners' Graveyards and all Spell/Trap Zones are empty. The fixture checks reverse chain resolution and that the Tokens are monsters, outside Heavy Storm's destruction scope. Chaining other Quick-Play Spells or Traps to Heavy Storm remains unaudited.

**Trap Dustshoot:** [The official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5446&ope=2&request_locale=en) requires at least four cards in the opponent's hand, shows that hand, and returns one chosen monster to the owner's Deck. A seeded native fixture Sets Dustshoot, activates it in the opponent's next Draw Phase while that opponent has six cards, and checks that the chooser's legal actions contain precisely the privately revealed monster identities, with no native hand slot numbers. The opponent receives no opponent-addressed hand reveal. Choosing a monster reduces the opponent's hand by one and increases its Deck count by one; the full duel then reaches terminal and its trajectory replay-verifies. The adapter admits a Dustshoot hand choice only when a recipient-addressed reveal arrived in the transition that produced it. A second native fixture shows Dustshoot absent from legal responses after the opponent Summons and Sets two cards, leaving three in hand; it becomes available again after that player draws to four. A third fixture follows the [historical competitive account of a blind Dustshoot that finds no monsters](https://www.goatformat.com/home/a-goat-format-world-championship-retrospective-prologue): it activates against a six-card Spell/Trap hand, reveals all six only to Dustshoot's controller, offers no return target, and leaves hand and Deck counts unchanged. The [historical Exchange ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) involving a card owned by the Dustshoot player remains untested.

**Dustshoot activation threshold during a chain:** [GOAT-specific rulings](https://goatrulings.com/goat-indv-rulings/trap-dustshoot.html) state that the opponent needs four hand cards when Dustshoot is activated; a chained Book of Moon or Mystical Space Typhoon that reduces the hand to three does not stop Dustshoot from resolving. One seeded native fixture has the opponent Summon a monster and Set one Book, leaving four cards. Dustshoot activates, the opponent chains a second Book from hand to turn that monster face-down, and Dustshoot then reveals the remaining three-card hand only to its controller and returns a selected monster to the Deck. A second fixture replaces the chained Book with Typhoon from hand targeting Dustshoot itself. Typhoon destroys Dustshoot, but its already activated effect still reveals the remaining three-card hand and returns a monster. Both tests check the hand count at activation, target choice, and resolution, plus the recipient-scoped reveal. Other effects that alter the hand during this chain remain untested.

**Royal Decree and Mirror Force:** [Royal Decree's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4866&ope=2&request_locale=en) negates other Trap effects on the field. In paired seeded native fixtures, one player Sets Decree and Normal Summons Archfiend Soldier while the opponent Sets Mirror Force. Mirror Force activates on the Soldier's direct attack in both branches. Without a response, it destroys Soldier and no battle damage occurs. When Decree is chained as Chain Link 2, it becomes face-up first, negates Mirror Force at Chain Link 1, and Soldier survives to deal 1900 direct damage. The pinned GOAT list permits Decree but forbids Imperial Order; its older optional upkeep therefore is outside the playable-card regression target for this profile. [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) allow it in the Battle Step but not the Damage Step, and distinguish bouncing the attacker (no replay) from bouncing the attack target (replay). Two seeded native fixtures Set Compulsory, target an opposing face-up monster, and resolve the effect. An ordinary Main Deck monster leaves the field and increases its owner's private hand count by one. In a Scapegoat/Metamorphosis line, Thousand-Eyes Restrict instead returns to its owner's Extra Deck; its hand count does not change. Neither target enters the Graveyard. Two more seeded Battle Step branches let the defender bounce the attacker or its own defending monster. Both return to their owners' private hands without battle damage; after the defending monster is bounced, the surviving attacker receives another attack choice. The bounced attacker cannot attack. A further fixture offers Compulsory in the Battle Step but removes it from legal responses after the attacked Dekoichi flips in the Damage Step, while the Trap remains set. Facedown targets, target removal in a chain, and control-changed targets remain untested.

**Thunder Dragon:** [Historical Netrep rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) allow discarding Thunder Dragon to search for zero copies, even when copies remain, and [the historical mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) gives the no-copy case as a Deck verification example. The pinned official script requires at least one copy in Deck, so GOAT AI uses the pinned GOAT variant `504700054`. A seeded native fixture offers a zero-card selection alongside one- and two-card choices while two copies remain; choosing zero leaves the Deck unchanged and reveals nothing to the opponent. A second fixture takes both copies and checks the opponent-only reveal, then discards another Thunder Dragon with no copies in Deck. That effect remains legal and reveals the remaining 32-card Deck to the opponent for verification. This checks the historical search behavior and information boundary; interaction with Exchange and banish replacement remains untested.

**Raigeki Break with Sinister Serpent:** [Official Raigeki Break text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5544&ope=2&request_locale=en) discards one card, targets one field card, and destroys it. The [Chaos Turbo guide](https://www.goatformat.com/chaos-turbo.html) identifies Raigeki Break and reusable discard fodder as important to that deck. A seeded native fixture Sets Raigeki Break, then activates it against an opponent's hidden monster. It chooses pre-errata Sinister Serpent from hand as the discard cost and confirms Serpent reaches the public Graveyard before target selection. The target action names only the opponent's monster zone and slot, without exposing its identity. Resolution destroys that monster, and Serpent returns during its controller's next Standby Phase. Chained removal of the target, activation with no discardable hand card, and Damage Step timing remain untested.

**Pre-errata Night Assailant:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) say that a discarded Night Assailant cannot select itself but can select another Night Assailant, including one discarded at the same time. The [current official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6042&ope=2&request_locale=en) excludes all Night Assailants, so GOAT AI uses the pinned pre-errata variant `16226796`. In a seeded native fixture, Graceful Charity discards both copies simultaneously. Both mandatory triggers appear; each chooses the other copy by its public Graveyard slot. Both return to hand, and the opponent receives two addressed card reveals without being shown the rest of the hand. A second fixture discards Magician of Faith with Graceful Charity, then discards Night Assailant as Raigeki Break's activation cost on a later turn. The target choice for Break sees Night Assailant already in the public Graveyard but still sees the opponent's set monster only by field slot. Break destroys that monster first; Night Assailant then targets Magician of Faith in the Graveyard, returns it to hand, and reveals it only to the opponent. Necrovalley interaction and recovery during other chain structures remain untested.

**Card Destruction with Night Assailant:** [Historical Card Destruction rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) say discarding is part of the effect rather than an activation cost, and [historical Night Assailant rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) explicitly permit its trigger when Card Destruction sends it from hand to Graveyard. A seeded native fixture activates Card Destruction with two pre-errata Night Assailants in hand. Both players discard five cards and privately draw five before either Assailant trigger asks for a target. The old hands are public in their respective Graveyards, while the new hand identities stay player-scoped. The two Assailants target each other in the Graveyard, return to hand, and become addressed reveals for the opponent. The empty-hand activation gate, deck-out edge cases, and chained hand changes remain untested.
