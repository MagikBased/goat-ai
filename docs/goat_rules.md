# GOAT rules profile

Target: TCG GOAT Format with April 2005 Forbidden/Limited list and the widely used August 17, 2005 card pool. The rule profile records first-turn draw and historical Ignition priority. Full validation is pending broader native regression coverage.

Sources: [GoatFormat mechanics](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html), [card pool and banlist](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html), and [ocgcore GOAT flags](https://github.com/edo9300/ygopro-core/blob/master/ocgapi_constants.h).

The M0 native probe confirms that a pinned core configured with `DUEL_MODE_GOAT` performs an opening draw for the first player in its vanilla-monster fixture. This is one startup behavior check, not a complete historical rules validation.

The M1 deck gate uses a pinned [Project Ignis GOAT whitelist](https://github.com/ProjectIgnis/LFLists/blob/master/GOAT.lflist.conf) and checks copy limits against the pinned main and [GOAT-variant card databases](https://github.com/ProjectIgnis/BabelCDB/blob/master/goat-entries.cdb). For example, the GOAT list allows two copies of Nobleman of Crossout under variant ID `504700116`; the ordinary ID `71044499` is intentionally rejected. [GoatFormat's card-pool guide](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html) also places Nobleman at two copies, and [its EDOPro guide](https://www.goatformat.com/home/what-is-edopro) explains the custom GOAT IDs. The independent membership comparison below checks the full pinned whitelist by card name.

**Independent staple-limit cross-check:** The [GoatFormat April 2005 list](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html) was transcribed into a regression for the 18 card identities that appear in at least four of the five sourced tournament Main Decks. The pinned GOAT whitelist agrees for all 18: thirteen have a one-copy limit, Nobleman of Crossout has a two-copy limit, and Book of Moon, Trap Dustshoot, Dekoichi, and Chaos Sorcerer allow three copies. Ten of these identities occur in every one of the five Main Decks. The test checks the GOAT and pre-errata IDs used by the fixtures, including BLS, Ring, Nobleman, and Sangan. This verifies those selected limits against an independent written list; it does not establish full August 2005 card-pool membership or all 1,685 pinned whitelist entries.

**Independent April 2005 copy-limit audit:** The [GoatFormat card-pool and banlist page](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html) names 17 Forbidden, 41 Limited, and 15 Semi-Limited cards. A pinned local HTML snapshot (SHA-256 `487b8b43e3810bebf0638f23b9c4abced26f0b796be2797ed7c427ee354ace1a`) is parsed by `scripts/audit_goat_limits.py`. It checks every one of the pinned whitelist's 1,704 IDs by normalized card name, treating names absent from those three restriction sections as unlimited. The measured comparison found all 73 restricted names in the whitelist and zero limit mismatches: 19 forbidden IDs, 43 limited IDs, 15 semi-limited IDs, and 1,627 unlimited IDs. The extra forbidden and limited IDs are alternate card IDs, not extra restricted names. This validates the pinned copy counts against that written list; it does not validate effects, historical text, or every set release date.

To reproduce after [native setup](native_setup.md), download the HTML into the ignored native cache and run:

```sh
curl -fsSL 'https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html' -o .cache/goat-ai/cardpool.html
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/audit_goat_limits.py .cache/goat-ai/cardpool.html --expect-page-sha256 487b8b43e3810bebf0638f23b9c4abced26f0b796be2797ed7c427ee354ace1a
```

The hash check rejects a changed page so that a future update requires a fresh comparison.

**Independent card-pool membership comparison:** The [GoatFormat card-pool page](https://www.goatformat.com/home/card-pool) links a public spreadsheet of cards listed as legal. A local read-only CSV snapshot (SHA-256 `445ae1c5511b0015f46a5727c8253f57b941501d9a64e26327fe5679994aee23`) contains 1,679 distinct names. `scripts/compare_goat_card_pool.py` matches those names against the pinned GOAT whitelist and card databases without committing the third-party spreadsheet. After ten explicit printed-name or spelling equivalences, all 1,679 spreadsheet names match. The whitelist has 1,685 allowed IDs representing 1,681 names; four names have two allowed card IDs. Two whitelist names are absent from the spreadsheet: Embodiment of Apophis and Robbin’ Zombie. The [GoatFormat cutoff](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html) explicitly includes the SP1 promo through Embodiment of Apophis; [Konami’s TCG database](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5920&ope=2) dates Robbin’ Zombie’s Invasion of Chaos print to March 2004, before the August 2005 cutoff. These are source-list omissions, so the deck gate retains both cards. The comparison checks name membership, not the historical accuracy of every effect or copy limit, and its ten name equivalences are recorded in the script for review.

**Deck sizes:** [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says the historical Main and Fusion Decks had no maximum size. The native adapter now accepts Main Decks with at least 40 cards and has a 61-card startup regression; larger decks have not been stress tested. The same guide says historical Side Decks were empty or exactly 15 cards. The default `RulesProfile` enforces that size rule. `RulesProfile(side_deck_policy="community")` allows 0–15 cards, reflecting the guide's note that short Side Decks are rarely disallowed in present-day online play. Sideboarding itself is not implemented.

The pinned Nobleman variant script targets a set monster and banishes it. The normal-monster regression confirms that the target stays unidentified in the opponent's legal action until it is revealed on resolution, then appears in the banished zone. This agrees with the [historical Nobleman ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) that the target is removed from play without going to the Graveyard. A second native fixture targets set Dekoichi (`87621407`), causing both players' remaining Decks to be shown for verification. [Historical tournament rulings](https://ms.yugipedia.com/a/a1/2003_tournament_rulings.pdf) require both players to show their Decks when a Flip Effect Monster is removed this way. The pinned core sends a separate `MSG_CONFIRM_CARDS` to each recipient; observations include only the addressed message, as a card multiset without native sequence numbers. The resulting duel reaches terminal state and its full schema-v10 trajectory replay-verifies. A third fixture targets set Magician of Faith with two more copies in each player's Deck and another copy in the activating player's hand. It verifies that Nobleman banishes the set target and all four Deck copies, leaves the hand copy intact, and sends no Magician of Faith to the Graveyard. This follows the same [historical card FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html), which restricts the additional removal to matching Deck copies. [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) notes that many online players skip deck verification to avoid persistent screenshots; the tested engine branch follows the reveal-and-verify ruling. Other Nobleman interactions remain unaudited.

**Nobleman hidden-target differential:** [Nobleman's historical FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) makes the Deck verification conditional on the targeted Set monster being a Flip Effect Monster. A paired seeded native fixture changes only the opponent's unrevealed Set monster between Dekoichi and a Normal Monster, then compares the Nobleman player's entire player-visible observation and legal target-action tuple immediately before target selection. They are identical, with no card code or known field identity. After resolution, both targets become public in the banished zone; only Dekoichi produces recipient-scoped Deck verification reveals. This is a direct hidden-information differential for a common removal card, beyond a check that one action omits a code. It does not prove all other private card changes are invisible.

**Book of Moon into Nobleman:** [Historical Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) allow it to turn a face-up monster face-down. [Nobleman's historical card FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) requires a face-down target and removes a non-Flip monster without a matching-Deck search. A seeded native regression has the opponent Normal Summon Breaker the Magical Warrior, then uses Book of Moon on Breaker and Nobleman on the resulting hidden field slot in the next Main Phase. Breaker is banished without entering the Graveyard, and no private Deck reveal occurs. This covers a common two-card removal line; chained responses and Breaker's own counter effect during this line remain untested.

**Book of Moon chained to Book of Moon:** [The historical Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) require a face-up monster as the target and establish that a chained Book can make a face-up-only target ineligible at resolution. A seeded native fixture has a player activate Book from hand on their face-up monster, then the opponent chains a previously Set Book targeting that same monster. The player-visible chain contains both Book links; after reverse resolution, the monster is face-down, each Book is in its owner's Graveyard, and neither player loses LP. The opponent's current field view hides the monster's identity while retaining its formerly public identity in field-slot memory. The first Book's lack of any further change is inferred from target ineligibility; the final field state alone cannot distinguish that from a redundant application. Book answered Book four times in the [random-agent response sample](competitive_action_coverage.md), which indicates decision exposure rather than historical play rate. Different-target chains remain untested.

**Book of Moon into Nobleman against Magician of Faith:** The same [historical Nobleman FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) says a targeted Flip Effect monster is banished without activating its Flip Effect, and matching copies in both Decks are banished after recipient-scoped Deck verification; cards in hand remain. A seeded native fixture has the opponent Normal Summon Faith face-up, uses Book to turn it face-down, then activates GOAT Nobleman. The attacker remembers Faith's formerly public identity in field memory but receives only an unidentified field slot in the legal target action. Nobleman banishes Faith and every matching copy still in the defender's Deck, offers no Faith trigger, and gives each player only their addressed Deck-verification reveal. This extends the non-Flip Breaker branch above to the Flip-monster branch of a commonly played Book/Nobleman line.

A second fixture follows the Pot-recovery pattern recorded in a [2005 championship match](https://www.goatformat.com/home/finals-robert-lim-vs-osman-ortiz): the defender activates Pot of Greed, Sets and later Flip Summons Faith, and recovers Pot without replaying it yet. The opponent sees Pot added to hand and remembers it, then uses Book to reset the now-public Faith and Nobleman to banish it plus matching Deck copies. Nobleman's target action still identifies only a field slot; the known Faith field entry disappears after banishment while the recovered Pot remains known in hand. This verifies one source-backed interruption to Faith reuse and the independent lifetimes of known field and hand information. Other responses to the recovery or Book activation remain untested.

**Solemn Judgment responding to Book of Moon:** [Solemn Judgment's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) permits paying half its controller's Life Points to negate and destroy an activated Spell. Paired seeded native fixtures Normal Summon a monster, Set Solemn opposite it, and activate Book on that face-up monster. Without a response, Book turns the monster face-down and the opponent retains its publicly seen identity only in field-slot memory. With Solemn chained to Book, its controller pays 4000 LP, Book reaches its owner's Graveyard with its activation negated, and the monster stays face-up; no hidden-card memory entry is needed. Both scripts load from the pinned official checkout. This pair appeared nine times as a selected chain response in the 60-duel [competitive action sample](competitive_action_coverage.md); that sample is a prioritization signal, not a historical ruling source.

**Solemn Judgment responding to Graceful Charity:** [Graceful's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4812&ope=2&request_locale=en) draws three cards and then discards two; [Solemn's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) negates a Spell activation after its controller pays half their Life Points. Paired seeded native fixtures offer Solemn as a response to Graceful while it is on the chain. If the defender declines, Graceful draws three private cards, presents its controller with the two-card discard choice, and ends with the original pre-activation hand count after discarding. If the defender activates Solemn, its controller falls to 4000 LP, Graceful reaches its owner's Graveyard, and the hand loses only the activated Spell: no draw or discard choice occurs and the Deck count is unchanged. The opponent receives no private draw reveal in either branch. This verifies one common hand-resource decision using pinned official scripts; further hand-trigger effects are covered separately.

**Solemn Judgment answering Solemn Judgment:** [Solemn's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) negates a Spell or Trap activation, and the [contemporary official rulebook's Spell Speed table](https://ms.yugipedia.com/2/25/Rulebook_v5.0.pdf) permits a Counter Trap to respond to another Counter Trap. A seeded native fixture has one player activate Pot of Greed, the opponent chain Solemn, and the Pot player chain a previously Set Solemn. Each Solemn controller pays 4000 LP at activation. The second Solemn negates the first; Pot then resolves and draws two cards. The three-link chain remains player-visible, the opponent's Set Solemn was hidden before activation, and the completed duel's schema-v18 trajectory replay-verifies against fresh pinned assets. The pair appeared six times in the [random-agent competitive-deck response sample](competitive_action_coverage.md); this is a decision-exposure signal, not a historical play-rate estimate. Longer Counter Trap chains remain untested.

**Solemn Judgment responding to Mystical Space Typhoon:** [Typhoon's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4909&ope=2&request_locale=en) targets and destroys one Spell or Trap; [historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) confirm that it targets. Paired seeded native fixtures Set Solemn and Book opposite a player holding Typhoon. Typhoon's legal target choices expose only the two hidden backrow slots. If Solemn is not used, Typhoon destroys the selected Book; Solemn remains set. If Solemn chains, its [activation negation](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) costs 4000 LP, Typhoon reaches its Graveyard, and Book stays set and unidentified to the Typhoon player. No private card reveal or known-card field entry is created. The pair appeared seven times as a selected response in the [competitive action sample](competitive_action_coverage.md); that sample is not historical play-rate evidence.

**Nobleman of Crossout versus Compulsory Evacuation Device:** [Tournament coverage](https://www.goatformat.com/home/round-four-rhymus-lizo-vs-toko-masaki) records Compulsory returning a set monster targeted by Nobleman to its owner’s hand. A seeded native fixture targets a hidden set Dekoichi with the pinned GOAT Nobleman script, then chains a previously Set Compulsory to return Dekoichi. Nobleman resolves without a target: neither Deck count changes, no Dekoichi is banished, and neither player receives a Deck reveal. The Nobleman player sees the opponent’s hand count increase but does not learn the identity of the previously hidden monster. Other target-removal responses remain untested.

**Solemn Judgment versus Compulsory Evacuation Device:** [Solemn's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) permits negating a Trap activation by paying half its controller's Life Points, and [historical Compulsory rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) allow returning an attacker during the Battle Step. Paired seeded native fixtures have Gemini Elf attack into a Set Compulsory while its controller has a previously Set Solemn. Without Solemn, Compulsory returns Elf to its owner's hand without damage; the opponent remembers the publicly seen card in that hand. With Solemn chained, its controller pays 4000 LP, Compulsory is negated and destroyed, and Elf remains face-up on the field rather than moving to the hand. Both official scripts load. The pair appeared six times in the [random-agent competitive-deck response sample](competitive_action_coverage.md); that sample is a prioritization signal, not a historical play-rate estimate. Follow-up attack damage and additional chain links remain untested.

**Solemn Judgment responding to Nobleman:** [Solemn's card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=en) permits paying half its controller's Life Points to negate a Spell activation; [Nobleman's historical FAQ](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) requires both Decks to be verified when its effect removes a Flip monster. Paired seeded native fixtures Set Magician of Faith and Solemn against Nobleman. Without a response, Nobleman banishes the set Faith and two remaining Faith copies from its owner's Deck, then sends recipient-scoped Deck verification reveals to both players. With Solemn chained to Nobleman, Solemn pays 4000 LP and negates the activation: Faith stays set and unidentified to the opponent, no Faith copy is banished, Deck counts stay fixed, and neither player receives a Deck reveal. The combination is verified against pinned GOAT Nobleman and official Solemn scripts. It appeared six times as a selected response pair in the 60-duel [competitive action sample](competitive_action_coverage.md); the sample does not establish a historical play rate.

**Ignition priority after a summon:** [GoatFormat's basic mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) states that after a Summon resolves, the turn player may activate a monster's Ignition Effect before the opponent may respond with a fast effect; its Cannon Soldier example uses this timing. The native regression test sets Book of Moon on the opponent's field, then Summons Cannon Soldier. After the summon-negation window, the core offers Cannon Soldier's Ignition Effect to the turn player. If that player activates it and tributes Cannon Soldier as cost, the opponent cannot use Book to target the absent monster; 500 damage resolves. If the turn player passes, the opponent receives a Book of Moon chain option and can turn Cannon Soldier facedown before its Ignition Effect is used. This tests one timing sequence, not the full priority system.

**Breaker summon trigger against Book of Moon:** [Historical Breaker rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) say its counter placement starts a chain after Normal Summon; the counter is unavailable until that chain resolves. The same rulings say Breaker receives no counter when Flip Summoned. [GoatFormat's priority guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) uses Breaker as the example where an opponent can respond to the placement trigger before Breaker's Ignition Effect becomes available. A seeded native fixture has the opponent chain a previously Set Book of Moon to that trigger. Breaker is face-down after the chain, with no Spell Counter and no immediately available destruction effect. When Flip Summoned on its controller's next turn, it remains at 1600 ATK with no counter or destruction effect. [Historical tournament coverage](https://www.goatformat.com/home/round-3-luke-chen-vs-cas-stomphorst) records the later window: Breaker targets a Set Book with its removal effect, and Book chains to flip Breaker face-down. A second seeded native fixture waits for Breaker’s counter-placement chain to resolve, removes its counter as the cost of targeting the hidden Book field slot, then chains Book onto Breaker. Breaker remains face-down, but its already activated effect still destroys Book. This also agrees with the [historical Breaker ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) that removing the counter pays the cost before a response. Other responses to the summon trigger remain unaudited.

**Breaker targeting a Set Scapegoat:** A [2005 tournament match report](https://www.goatformat.com/home/round-3-luke-chen-vs-cas-stomphorst) records Breaker removing its counter to target Scapegoat, which its controller chains. Paired seeded native fixtures wait for Breaker's counter-placement chain to finish, pay that counter as the destruction cost, and select the opponent's hidden Spell/Trap slot without exposing its card identity. If the defender passes, Breaker destroys Scapegoat and no Tokens appear. If the defender chains it, four Sheep Tokens appear before Breaker destroys the still-activated Scapegoat; the Tokens remain and neither player loses Life Points. This tests a frequent backrow-response decision and its information boundary under the pinned GOAT scripts. Additional chains to Scapegoat, including effect negation, remain separate cases.

**Breaker summon trigger against Enemy Controller:** The [November 2004 UDE FAQ change log](https://www.cogonline.net/threads/official-ude-faq-change-log-updated-18-jan-2005-1130-cst.37856/) places Breaker's Spell Counter after a chained Enemy Controller takes control of it. A seeded native fixture has the defender Normal Summon a monster and Set Controller, then chains Controller to the attacker's Breaker counter-placement trigger. Choosing Controller's control mode Tributes the defender's monster as cost and targets Breaker. Controller resolves first; Breaker’s pending trigger then puts a counter on the now-stolen monster, which has 1900 ATK after the chain. The original Summoner has no Breaker destruction action at that decision. The fixture also follows the End Phase return: Breaker moves back to its original controller with the Spell Counter and 1900 ATK intact at the next turn's Main Phase. It verifies the pinned official scripts and both players' Life Points. Alternate chain responses remain untested.

**Breaker summon trigger against Bottomless Trap Hole:** [The historical Breaker and Bottomless rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) allow Bottomless to chain to Breaker's counter-placement effect after its Normal Summon. Its current ATK is 1600, so Bottomless is legal before the counter would raise it to 1900. A seeded native fixture Sets GOAT-variant Bottomless, Normal Summons Breaker on the following turn, confirms Breaker has 1600 ATK and no counter at the response prompt, then chains Bottomless. Breaker is banished before the counter can be placed or its removal effect can be used. This checks one common summon-response branch; other counter placement interruptions remain untested.

**Field Spell replacement:** [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says only one face-up Field Spell can remain active; when a new Field Spell activation resolves, the previous one is destroyed. One native test activates pre-errata Necrovalley (`511002998`) for player 0, then A Legendary Ocean (`295517`) for player 1. At the next turn, only Ocean remains in the Field Zone and Necrovalley is in player 0's Graveyard. A second test sets Mystical Space Typhoon, then chains it to Ocean's activation and destroys Ocean before resolution; Necrovalley remains face-up. Other field interactions remain untested.

**Flip Effect:** The [official Dekoichi card database entry](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6201&ope=2&request_locale=en) states that its Flip Effect draws one card, plus one per face-up Bokoichi controlled. A native regression sets Dekoichi (`87621407`), Flip Summons it on the next turn, and verifies one card moves from deck to hand with no Bokoichi present. A second native fixture draws with Dekoichi, uses Book of Moon to turn it face-down, verifies the opponent cannot see its identity in the field observation and it cannot be Flip Summoned again that turn, then Flip Summons it on a later turn for a second effect draw. [GoatFormat's Flip Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/flip-control.html) lists Dekoichi and Book of Moon together, and the [historical Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) describe turning Flip monsters face-down. The [GoatFormat Dekoichi rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) add card-specific cases involving Bokoichi at resolution; those cases are not yet covered.

**Cyber Jar:** [GoatFormat's Cyber Jar rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) require both players to reveal their five cards before either starts placing monsters; the turn player places theirs first. A native regression with GOAT variant `504700188` verifies both public Deck-top reveals are available at the first placement decision, followed by five turn-player and five opponent placement choices. It confirms that an opponent monster placed face-down has no identity in the acting player's field observation. The reveal records retain the publicly shown card multiset; they do not identify a shuffled face-down field slot. Other Cyber Jar branches and card interactions remain unaudited.

**Ground Collapse:** [The official Ground Collapse card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5170&ope=2) says its controller chooses two unoccupied Monster Zones and neither player may use them. A pinned native fixture activates `90502999`, chooses its own Monster Zones 0 and 1 in a single six-byte response, and confirms the next Normal Summon can be placed only in Zones 2, 3, or 4. The public disabled-zone message populates both the agent observation and replay viewer. Removing Ground Collapse and interactions with other zone restrictions still need native regressions.

**Question:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5494&ope=2&request_locale=ae) forbids the guessing player from checking the activating player's Graveyard when naming its bottom monster. A seeded native fixture reaches the `MSG_ANNOUNCE_CARD` prompt: the adapter offers Monster names from the pinned GOAT whitelist, hides identities in the activating player's Graveyard for that decision, and a correct announcement banishes the bottom monster. The catalog includes Forbidden cards because a name announcement is not a Deck inclusion. It expands GOAT-variant aliases to their original printed-name IDs, then excludes alias records and Tokens rejected by the pinned core's declaration expression. In a second seeded fixture, naming Harpie Lady by its original ID (`76812113`) correctly banishes the GOAT-variant card (`54415063`); the core's `GetCode()` resolves that variant to its alias. The catalog has not been independently checked against every card name available by August 2005, so complete historical name legality remains unverified. [Historical Question rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) also cover Special Summon restrictions that remain untested.

**Exchange:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5218&ope=2&request_locale=en) says both players reveal their hands and each adds one card from the other's hand. A seeded native fixture activates `5556668`; the core sends one hand reveal to each recipient before their selection. Each action exposes only card names in that recipient's reveal, without the opponent hand's native slot index. Both selected cards move to the new controller's hand. The other player's observation receives only their own addressed reveal. [Historical Exchange rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) also forbid activation when Exchange is its controller's only hand card; that branch remains untested.

**Fruits of Kozaky's Studies:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6220&ope=2&request_locale=en) lets its controller look at the top three Deck cards and return them in any order. A native fixture offers all six `MSG_SORT_CARD` permutations, verifies that a reversed choice changes the Deck order, and confirms no Deck-top reveal reaches the opponent. This covers one sorting effect; further card-specific work will prioritize frequently played GOAT cards.

**Tsukuyomi and Dekoichi repeat Flip:** [The historical Tsukuyomi ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) makes its face-down effect mandatory, and [the Flip Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/flip-control.html) identifies Tsukuyomi with Dekoichi as a recurring draw line. A seeded native fixture Sets Dekoichi, Flip Summons it to draw, then Normal Summons Tsukuyomi and targets Dekoichi with its mandatory effect. Dekoichi is hidden from the opponent and cannot Flip Summon again that turn. Tsukuyomi returns to its controller’s hand at the End Phase; on a later turn, Dekoichi Flip Summons and draws again. A paired fixture Normal Summons Tsukuyomi as the only face-up monster. Its mandatory effect offers only itself as a target, turns itself face-down, hides its current identity from the opponent while retaining public field memory, and leaves it on the field through the End Phase. This follows the [historical self-target and face-up Spirit return rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html). Chained interruption of the mandatory target remains untested.

**Book of Moon chained to Tsukuyomi:** Paired seeded native fixtures add an opponent's Set Book to the Dekoichi repeat-Flip line. Tsukuyomi targets face-up Dekoichi with its mandatory trigger. If Book turns Tsukuyomi face-down, the pending trigger still sets Dekoichi; both monsters stay hidden, and Tsukuyomi remains on the field at the next turn boundary. The opponent's field-slot memory retains both previously face-up identities. If Book instead sets Dekoichi, Tsukuyomi's target is no longer face-up: Dekoichi remains set, while Tsukuyomi stays face-up until it returns to hand in the End Phase. A seeded pinned-native fixture uses Graceful Charity to put LIGHT and DARK monsters in the original owner's Graveyard, Special Summons BLS, then has the opponent take it with Snatch. The new controller is offered BLS's effect, banishes the original owner's face-up monster, and is not offered a BLS attack in the following Battle Phase. The control-and-effect combination is an inference from those rules and is verified against the pinned scripts; effects used before a control change and chained removal of Snatch remain separate cases.

**Tsukuyomi resetting Gravekeeper's Spy:** A [Gravekeeper strategy guide](https://www.goatformat.com/gravekeeper.html) describes using Tsukuyomi to reuse Spy, and [historical Tsukuyomi rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) make its face-down-setting effect mandatory. A seeded pinned-native fixture Sets GOAT-variant Spy, Flip Summons it to recruit Guard, then Normal Summons Tsukuyomi and targets Spy. Spy becomes face-down; the opponent sees an unidentified field card while retaining its previously public identity by slot. Tsukuyomi returns to its owner's hand at End Phase. On the next turn, Spy can Flip Summon again and recruit a second Guard. The fixture covers this two-turn loop and visible information, not interruption or exhausted-Deck branches.

**Tsukuyomi and Magician of Faith reuse Pot of Greed:** The [Flip Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/flip-control.html) identifies Tsukuyomi plus Flip Effect monsters as a repeated-card-advantage engine; a [historical feature match](https://www.goatformat.com/home/finals-robert-lim-vs-osman-ortiz) records Magician of Faith recovering Pot multiple times and then being set by Tsukuyomi. The opponent sees the field card face-down while retaining its formerly public identity. Tsukuyomi returns to hand at End Phase; on the following turn Faith recovers Pot again and the same physical Pot is activated for a third time. Both recoveries reveal Pot to the opponent and mark it as known in hand until activation removes it. The fixture checks the resulting eight-card Deck decrease from two regular draws and three two-card Pot resolutions after the opening observation. It does not cover interruptions to the loop or Deck-out.

**Chaos Sorcerer priority versus Bottomless Trap Hole:** [GOAT's Ignition-priority rule](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) lets the turn player activate an Ignition Effect immediately after a Summon before the opponent responds; [historical Bottomless rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) place its removal after the Summon. At the priority choice, the Sorcerer player's view contains an unidentified set Trap. If that player activates Sorcerer's banish effect, Bottomless chains and banishes Sorcerer, then the already activated Sorcerer effect banishes its target. If the player passes priority, Bottomless banishes Sorcerer and the opposing monster survives. Both branches spend the LIGHT/DARK Summon cost, then finish as full seeded random duels whose visible trajectories serialize and replay-verify. This tests an inferred combination of the documented timing rules against the pinned GOAT core and scripts; other summon responses and effect-negation chains remain unaudited.

**Delinquent Duo discarding Night Assailant:** [Duo's historical Netrep ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) requires both discards to finish before an effect triggered by the first discard starts a new Chain. A seeded native fixture has the defender discard Magician of Faith to Graceful Charity, then the attacker activates Duo on the following turn. Duo's random first discard hits a different Night Assailant; while Duo remains on the Chain, the defender still chooses a second hand card to discard and receives no Night recovery choice or private reveal. Only after Duo resolves does Night select Faith from the Graveyard and return it to hand, revealing Faith to the attacker. The attacker remembers Faith as known in hand, while the other cards in the defender's hand remain hidden. This covers the seeded random outcome and one recovery target, not every random discard or replacement effect.

**Delinquent Duo after a Thunder Dragon search:** [GOAT's hand and Deck verification rule](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) and [historical Thunder Dragon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) support the GOAT script revealing two searched copies to the opponent; a [Chaos Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/home/a-guide-to-chaos-control) identifies Thunder Dragon as protection against Duo discards. Two pinned-native seeds discard the first Thunder Dragon as its own activation cost, search the other two, and publicly Normal Summon a different monster. Duo then randomly discards either an unknown card or one searched Thunder Dragon; in both branches the defender chooses a remaining Thunder Dragon for its second discard. The attacker's known-hand multiset retains two, one, or zero Thunder Dragon copies as the public hand changes warrant. The set-monster alternative is intentionally separate: a hidden Set could be either known copy, so it reduces the number of copies guaranteed to remain before Duo.

**Mirror Force versus Sakuretsu Armor choice:** [Historical Mirror Force rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) describe its non-targeting removal of face-up Attack Position monsters, whereas [historical Sakuretsu rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) make it target the attacking monster. The defender's legal action list contains both Trap activations at the same Battle Step. Choosing Mirror destroys both attackers; choosing Sakuretsu destroys only the declared attacker and leaves the other monster on the field. The attacker's observation hides both set Trap identities before activation. Both branches then finish with seeded random agents; their complete visible trajectories serialize and replay-verify against the pinned engine. This checks alternative legal responses and resulting field states, not simultaneous activation of both Traps or other attack modifiers.

**Trap Dustshoot chained to Thunder Dragon:** [Dustshoot reveals the opponent's hand and returns one monster to its owner's Deck](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5446&ope=2&request_locale=en), while [Thunder Dragon's historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) permit its activated discard effect to search for up to two copies. Dustshoot reveals the current hand to its controller and returns a selected monster, then Thunder Dragon searches its other two copies. The opponent's known-hand multiset contains the remaining Dustshoot-revealed cards and both searched Thunder Dragons; the searcher's observation gains no knowledge of the Dustshoot controller's hand. This covers this two-link chain and recipient-scoped hand knowledge, not every simultaneous search or hand-changing effect.

**Call of the Haunted reviving Sangan before Mystical Space Typhoon resolves:** [Historical Call rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) distinguish Typhoon destroying Call before its activation resolves from Call chaining to Typhoon and reviving its monster first. **Black Luster Soldier reset by Book of Moon:** [Historical BLS rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) explicitly allow its banish effect again in the same turn after BLS is turned face-down and face-up. **Magician of Faith reusing Graceful Charity:** [Faith's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4434&ope=2&request_locale=en) returns a targeted Spell from the Graveyard to hand, and [Graceful Charity's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4812&ope=2&request_locale=en) draws three cards before two discards. The opponent receives no reveal of the private draws. This covers one unopposed reuse of Graceful; interruption chains and discard-trigger effects are separate cases.
