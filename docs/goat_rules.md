# GOAT rules profile

Target: TCG GOAT Format with April 2005 Forbidden/Limited list and the widely used August 17, 2005 card pool. The rule profile records first-turn draw and historical Ignition priority. Full validation is pending broader native regression coverage.

Sources: [GoatFormat mechanics](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html), [card pool and banlist](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html), and [ocgcore GOAT flags](https://github.com/edo9300/ygopro-core/blob/master/ocgapi_constants.h).

The M0 native probe confirms that a pinned core configured with `DUEL_MODE_GOAT` performs an opening draw for the first player in its vanilla-monster fixture. This is one startup behavior check, not a complete historical rules validation.

The M1 deck gate uses a pinned [Project Ignis GOAT whitelist](https://github.com/ProjectIgnis/LFLists/blob/master/GOAT.lflist.conf) and checks copy limits against the pinned main and [GOAT-variant card databases](https://github.com/ProjectIgnis/BabelCDB/blob/master/goat-entries.cdb). For example, the GOAT list allows two copies of Nobleman of Crossout under variant ID `504700116`; the ordinary ID `71044499` is intentionally rejected. [GoatFormat's card-pool guide](https://www.goatformat.com/cardpool.html) also places Nobleman at two copies, and [its EDOPro guide](https://www.goatformat.com/home/what-is-edopro) explains the custom GOAT IDs. Full membership still needs an independent comparison with the August 17, 2005 card pool.

**Deck sizes:** [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says the historical Main and Fusion Decks had no maximum size. The native adapter now accepts Main Decks with at least 40 cards and has a 61-card startup regression; larger decks have not been stress tested. The same guide says historical Side Decks were empty or exactly 15 cards. The default `RulesProfile` enforces that size rule. `RulesProfile(side_deck_policy="community")` allows 0–15 cards, reflecting the guide's note that short Side Decks are rarely disallowed in present-day online play. Sideboarding itself is not implemented.

The pinned Nobleman variant script targets a set monster and banishes it. The normal-monster regression confirms that the target stays unidentified in the opponent's legal action until it is revealed on resolution, then appears in the banished zone. This agrees with the [historical Nobleman ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) that the target is removed from play without going to the Graveyard. A second native fixture targets set Dekoichi (`87621407`), causing both players' remaining Decks to be shown for verification. [Historical tournament rulings](https://ms.yugipedia.com/a/a1/2003_tournament_rulings.pdf) require both players to show their Decks when a Flip Effect Monster is removed this way. The pinned core sends a separate `MSG_CONFIRM_CARDS` to each recipient; observations include only the addressed message, as a card multiset without native sequence numbers. The resulting duel reaches terminal state and its full schema-v10 trajectory replay-verifies. [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) notes that many online players skip deck verification to avoid persistent screenshots; the tested engine branch follows the reveal-and-verify ruling. Other Nobleman interactions remain unaudited.

**Ignition priority after a summon:** [GoatFormat's basic mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) states that after a Summon resolves, the turn player may activate a monster's Ignition Effect before the opponent may respond with a fast effect; its Cannon Soldier example uses this timing. The native regression test sets Book of Moon on the opponent's field, then Summons Cannon Soldier. After the summon-negation window, the core offers Cannon Soldier's Ignition Effect to the turn player. If that player activates it and tributes Cannon Soldier as cost, the opponent cannot use Book to target the absent monster; 500 damage resolves. If the turn player passes, the opponent receives a Book of Moon chain option and can turn Cannon Soldier facedown before its Ignition Effect is used. This tests one timing sequence, not the full priority system.

**Field Spell replacement:** [GoatFormat's mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says only one face-up Field Spell can remain active; when a new Field Spell activation resolves, the previous one is destroyed. One native test activates pre-errata Necrovalley (`511002998`) for player 0, then A Legendary Ocean (`295517`) for player 1. At the next turn, only Ocean remains in the Field Zone and Necrovalley is in player 0's Graveyard. A second test sets Mystical Space Typhoon, then chains it to Ocean's activation and destroys Ocean before resolution; Necrovalley remains face-up. Other field interactions remain untested.

**Flip Effect:** The [official Dekoichi card database entry](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6201&ope=2&request_locale=en) states that its Flip Effect draws one card, plus one per face-up Bokoichi controlled. A native regression sets Dekoichi (`87621407`), Flip Summons it on the next turn, and verifies one card moves from deck to hand with no Bokoichi present. The [GoatFormat Dekoichi rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) add card-specific cases involving Bokoichi at resolution; those cases are not yet covered.

**Cyber Jar:** [GoatFormat's Cyber Jar rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) require both players to reveal their five cards before either starts placing monsters; the turn player places theirs first. A native regression with GOAT variant `504700188` verifies both public Deck-top reveals are available at the first placement decision, followed by five turn-player and five opponent placement choices. It confirms that an opponent monster placed face-down has no identity in the acting player's field observation. The reveal records retain the publicly shown card multiset; they do not identify a shuffled face-down field slot. Other Cyber Jar branches and card interactions remain unaudited.

**Ground Collapse:** [The official Ground Collapse card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5170&ope=2) says its controller chooses two unoccupied Monster Zones and neither player may use them. A pinned native fixture activates `90502999`, chooses its own Monster Zones 0 and 1 in a single six-byte response, and confirms the next Normal Summon can be placed only in Zones 2, 3, or 4. The public disabled-zone message populates both the agent observation and replay viewer. Removing Ground Collapse and interactions with other zone restrictions still need native regressions.

**Question:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5494&ope=2&request_locale=ae) forbids the guessing player from checking the activating player's Graveyard when naming its bottom monster. A seeded native fixture reaches the `MSG_ANNOUNCE_CARD` prompt: the adapter offers Monster names from the pinned GOAT whitelist, hides identities in the activating player's Graveyard for that decision, and a correct announcement banishes the bottom monster. The catalog includes Forbidden cards because a name announcement is not a Deck inclusion. It expands GOAT-variant aliases to their original printed-name IDs, then excludes alias records and Tokens rejected by the pinned core's declaration expression. In a second seeded fixture, naming Harpie Lady by its original ID (`76812113`) correctly banishes the GOAT-variant card (`54415063`); the core's `GetCode()` resolves that variant to its alias. The catalog has not been independently checked against every card name available by August 2005, so complete historical name legality remains unverified. [Historical Question rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) also cover Special Summon restrictions that remain untested.

**Exchange:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5218&ope=2&request_locale=en) says both players reveal their hands and each adds one card from the other's hand. A seeded native fixture activates `5556668`; the core sends one hand reveal to each recipient before their selection. Each action exposes only card names in that recipient's reveal, without the opponent hand's native slot index. Both selected cards move to the new controller's hand. The other player's observation receives only their own addressed reveal. [Historical Exchange rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) also forbid activation when Exchange is its controller's only hand card; that branch remains untested.

**Fruits of Kozaky's Studies:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=6220&ope=2&request_locale=en) lets its controller look at the top three Deck cards and return them in any order. A native fixture offers all six `MSG_SORT_CARD` permutations, verifies that a reversed choice changes the Deck order, and confirms no Deck-top reveal reaches the opponent. This covers one sorting effect; further card-specific work will prioritize frequently played GOAT cards.

**Damage Step activation:** [GoatFormat's historical mechanics guide](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) distinguishes GOAT Damage Step activation windows from current rules. A native regression attacks a set Dekoichi while its controller has a set Book of Moon. The core offers Book in the attack response window. Once Dekoichi flips face-up during the Damage Step, Book remains set but is no longer a legal chain choice; Dekoichi's Flip Effect subsequently draws one card. This covers one activation restriction and one Flip Effect timing sequence, not every Damage Step substep. The adapter labels `battle_step` and `damage_step` from public core events. It does not expose the six historical Damage Step substeps individually.

**Missed timing:** The [historical Peten the Dark Clown rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) say its optional “when… you can” trigger cannot activate when Cannon Soldier tributes it as a cost, because Cannon Soldier's effect resolves afterward. A native regression summons Cannon Soldier and Peten, tributes Peten for 500 damage, and verifies that no Peten trigger is offered while two other copies remain in the Deck. A positive control destroys a set Peten in battle, accepts its trigger, banishes it as cost, and Special Summons another copy. These cases validate this specific optional trigger; broader effects triggered mid-chain remain open.

**Trigger ordering:** [GoatFormat's historical SEGOC example](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) says that when Sangan is tributed to Summon Thestalos, Sangan's mandatory trigger is chain link 1 and Thestalos's mandatory trigger is chain link 2. A native fixture performs that Tribute Summon and verifies both visible chain links. Thestalos resolves first, discarding from the opponent's hand, followed by Sangan adding a monster from the Deck to its controller's hand. This covers one two-trigger sequence. The Soul Exchange variant and triggers recognized mid-chain remain untested.

**Summon negation:** [GoatFormat's Solemn Judgment rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) distinguish negating a Summon from negating a Special Summon produced by another card effect. The [official Solemn Judgment card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4861&ope=2&request_locale=ae) says its activation pays half the controller's Life Points and negates a Summon. A pinned native regression sets Solemn Judgment, attempts a Normal Summon of Cannon Soldier, and chains Solemn in the summon-negation window. Player 1 goes from 8000 to 4000 LP; Cannon Soldier is destroyed and sent to player 0's Graveyard without reaching the field, no Ignition Effect is available, and the Normal Summon opportunity is spent. This tests one Normal Summon branch, not effect-based Special Summons or summon-negation triggers.

**Pre-errata Dark Magician of Chaos:** [GoatFormat's historical card text and rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) say its optional effect can add a Spell from the Graveyard when Normal Summoned, and that it is banished when it leaves the field face-up but goes to the Graveyard if it leaves face-down. A pinned native regression first resolves Pot of Greed, Summons two normal monsters over two turns, then Tributes both to Normal Summon pre-errata Dark Magician of Chaos (`511001039`). The player accepts its effect, targets Pot in the Graveyard, and returns Pot to hand. Two more native cases use Cannon Soldier to tribute it from a face-up or Book of Moon face-down position and verify the respective destination before Cannon Soldier resolves. The tests confirm the pre-errata script was loaded. Special Summons, missed timing, and other ways of leaving the field remain untested.

**Scapegoat:** [GoatFormat's historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) say that a player cannot activate Scapegoat after Summoning that turn, cannot Summon afterward, may still Set a monster, and may Tribute a Sheep Token for purposes other than a Tribute Summon. They also require four vacant Monster Card Zones and distinguish negating Scapegoat's activation, which restores Summon permission, from negating only its effect. Pinned native regressions activate the GOAT variant (`504700123`) before Summoning, choose four token zones, and verify four face-up Defense Position tokens, no available Summon, and an available monster Set. The public native player hint appears in both players' observations as `player0:cannot_summon_this_turn:scapegoat` while active and disappears at turn end. A separate branch Normal Summons first and verifies Scapegoat is unavailable that turn. Another branch reaches a later Main Phase with two monsters already controlled: Scapegoat can be Set but cannot be activated despite Normal Summons still being legal. A token branch keeps the four tokens until the next turn, verifies that they do not make a two-Tribute Dark Magician of Chaos Normal Summon legal, then Tributes one for Cannon Soldier's effect: it disappears without entering a Graveyard or banished zone, and Cannon Soldier deals 500 damage. A Solemn Judgment branch negates Scapegoat's activation: the hint appears during the chain, then disappears; no Tokens appear and a Normal Summon is again legal that turn. Negating only the effect and other token interactions remain untested.

**Goat Control Metamorphosis line:** [GoatFormat's Goat Control guide](https://www.goatformat.com/goat-control.html) identifies Scapegoat, Metamorphosis, Thousand-Eyes Restrict, and Tsukuyomi as a central interaction. [Metamorphosis's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5539&ope=2) Tributes a monster to Special Summon a Fusion Monster of the same Level. A pinned fixture activates Scapegoat, confirms that Metamorphosis cannot be activated after its tokens appear that turn, then on a later turn Tributes one Sheep Token to Special Summon GOAT-variant Thousand-Eyes Restrict (`504700102`) from the Extra Deck. The token does not enter a Graveyard. Restrict equips the opponent's face-up monster, consistent with [its official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4740&ope=2). [Tsukuyomi's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5780&ope=2&request_locale=en) turns a face-up monster face-down; the fixture Normal Summons it, turns Restrict face-down, confirms the equipped monster reaches its owner's Graveyard, and checks that the opponent sees the set Restrict without its identity. Repeated Flip Summon and re-equip loops, other Fusion targets, and interruption branches remain untested.

**Graceful Charity and Delinquent Duo:** [Graceful Charity's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4812&ope=2&request_locale=en) draws three cards, then discards two. [Delinquent Duo's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4901&ope=2&request_locale=en) pays 1000 LP, makes the opponent discard a random card, then lets that opponent choose one of their remaining cards to discard. A pinned native fixture activates both in one duel. It verifies all 28 two-card choices from Graceful's eight-card hand, the three-card draw and two-card discard, Duo's LP payment and random first discard, and four choices offered only to Duo's opponent for their second discard. Both observers see only the opponent's hand count throughout. This covers the ordinary full-hand branch; interactions with replacement effects and hands too small to resolve remain untested.

**Snatch Steal, Heavy Storm, and Book of Moon:** [Snatch Steal's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4898&ope=2&request_locale=en) takes control of the equipped opponent monster and gives that opponent 1000 LP during their Standby Phase. [Heavy Storm's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4891&ope=2&request_locale=en) destroys all Spells and Traps on the field. [Historical Snatch Steal rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) say the monster returns to its previous controller when Snatch Steal leaves the field, except that turning the monster face-down sends Snatch Steal to the Graveyard and leaves the monster with Snatch Steal's controller. One seeded native fixture steals an opponent monster, verifies the opponent's 1000 LP recovery, and destroys Snatch Steal with Heavy Storm; the monster returns to its original controller. A second fixture activates [Book of Moon](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5432&ope=2&request_locale=en) on the stolen monster: Snatch Steal goes to the Graveyard, the face-down monster remains under the stealing player's control, and its original controller sees an unidentified face-down field card with the previously public card identity retained in field memory. Other control-changing effects and chained interruptions remain untested.

**Mirror Force and Book of Moon:** [Mirror Force's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4887&ope=2&request_locale=en) destroys the opponent's Attack Position monsters when one declares an attack. [Historical Mirror Force rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) specify that a monster turned face-down in the chain survives. A seeded native fixture Normal Summons two monsters over two turns, attacks into a set Mirror Force, and chains Book of Moon on the attacking monster. The attacker survives face-down, the other Attack Position monster is destroyed, and neither player loses LP. This covers one attack-response chain; replay timing and other battle interruptions remain untested.

**Torrential Tribute and Book of Moon:** [Torrential Tribute's official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5114&ope=2&request_locale=en) destroys all monsters after a Summon. [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) place its activation after the Summon rather than negating the Summon, and exclude Damage Step Summons. A seeded native fixture sets Torrential Tribute, Normal Summons a second monster on a later turn, then chains Book of Moon to Torrential Tribute. The Book target turns face-down, but both monsters are destroyed when Torrential resolves. The opposing set Trap identity stays hidden before activation. This covers a Normal Summon response; Flip and Special Summons, Damage Step exclusion, and priority-sensitive effect activations remain to be checked with separate fixtures.

**Pre-errata Ring of Destruction:** [GoatFormat's historical card text and rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) say Ring can destroy a face-up monster and deals damage equal to its current ATK to both players; it cannot activate during the Damage Step. The pinned assets contain pre-errata variant `511000824`, whose script is distinct from the modern `83555666` script. A seeded native fixture confirms that the pre-errata script was loaded, sets Ring, targets its controller's own Gemini Elf during the opponent's Draw Phase, destroys it, and reduces both players from 8000 to 6100 LP. The opponent sees only an unidentified set Trap before activation. A second fixture follows the [historical Breaker interaction](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html): Breaker removes its Spell Counter as the cost of targeting a set Book of Moon; Ring chains to destroy Breaker and deals 1600 damage to each player; Breaker's effect still destroys Book on resolution. The Breaker player selects only a hidden field slot, not the set card identity. Lethal damage, Damage Step exclusion, and further chained target removal remain untested. Schema v12 now exposes current ATK, DEF, and counters for face-up monsters, including Breaker before and after its cost; other visible game-state details still need audit.

**Chaos Sorcerer:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5833&ope=2&request_locale=en) requires banishing a LIGHT and DARK monster from the Graveyard to first Special Summon it, then permits a once-per-turn face-up monster banish at the cost of its attack that turn. [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) add that the effect is limited to the controller's Main Phase and that a Book of Moon response can make its target invalid while still consuming the effect and attack opportunity. In a seeded native fixture, Graceful Charity discards one LIGHT and one DARK monster, Chaos Sorcerer banishes those two cards from the Graveyard as its summon cost, and its effect banishes an opposing face-up monster. No second activation or attack with Sorcerer is offered that turn. A negative fixture has the same Attributes only on the field with an empty Graveyard and confirms no Chaos Sorcerer Special Summon is legal. A separate branch chains Book of Moon to turn the targeted opposing monster face-down: Sorcerer does not banish it, cannot activate again or attack that turn, and the formerly public target remains in field-slot known-card memory without exposing its face-down identity. Main Phase 2 and other cost combinations remain untested.

**Black Luster Soldier - Envoy of the Beginning:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) allow its second attack after it destroys a monster in battle, including a direct attack if the first target was the opponent's only monster; the optional trigger occurs during the first attack's Damage Step. Activating its Main Phase banish effect prevents BLS from attacking that turn. Seeded native fixtures use the GOAT variant script (`504700118`), discard a LIGHT and DARK monster with Graceful Charity, and banish those monsters from the Graveyard to Special Summon BLS. One branch destroys the opponent's sole monster, accepts the Damage Step trigger, attacks directly for 3000, and finds no third attack. Another branch banishes the opponent's face-up monster with BLS's first effect, then finds neither a second activation nor a BLS attack in the Battle Phase. A third branch confirms that BLS's banish effect is available before attacking, declines the optional second attack after battle destruction, and finds the banish effect unavailable in Main Phase 2 while BLS remains face-up. Self-targeting, effect negation, and negated attacks remain untested.

**Sinister Serpent:** [Historical card text and rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) make its Graveyard-to-hand return optional during its controller's Standby Phase, regardless of how it entered the Graveyard. The native fixture uses the pre-errata variant (`511000818`), discards it through Graceful Charity, and verifies that the controller is offered both accepting and declining the return at the next Standby Phase. Accepting moves Serpent to the hand; declining leaves it in the Graveyard. The opponent sees the public Graveyard card and hand count without receiving the private hand contents. Same-Standby-Phase reactivation after a new discard and suppression by Dark Ruler Ha Des or The End of Anubis remain untested.

**Tribe-Infecting Virus and Sinister Serpent:** [Historical Tribe rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) require discarding as the activation cost and declaring a Type before the opponent can chain; only a Type currently represented by a face-up monster can be declared. A seeded native fixture Normal Summons Tribe (`33184167`), discards pre-errata Sinister Serpent as the cost, and offers only Aqua (Tribe) and Reptile (the opponent's Gagagigo) in the Type announcement. Declaring Reptile destroys Gagagigo but leaves Tribe on the field. Serpent stays in the public Graveyard until the controller accepts its optional return at the next Standby Phase. A second native branch has the opponent chain a set Book of Moon after the Reptile announcement to turn Gagagigo face-down. Tribe resolves after Book and does not destroy the now face-down monster, while Serpent remains in the Graveyard as the already-paid cost. The acting player sees a hidden face-down field identity with public memory of the previously face-up Gagagigo; the owner still sees its own card. Chained DNA Surgery Type changes, multiple monsters of one Type, and repeated Tribe activations remain untested.

**Premature Burial:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings6.html) require paying 800 LP and targeting a monster in the Graveyard, and [Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) say that turning its revived monster face-down sends Premature Burial to the Graveyard while the monster remains on the field. In paired seeded native fixtures, Graceful Charity discards Mystical Elf, Premature Burial pays 800 LP and revives it, then either Book of Moon flips it face-down or Mystical Space Typhoon destroys Premature Burial. Book leaves the face-down monster on the field and the opponent's current field view hides its identity while retaining memory of the previously public card. Mystical Space Typhoon's destruction of Premature Burial also destroys the revived monster. A third fixture chains Mystical Space Typhoon to Premature Burial's initial activation after its Graveyard target and 800 LP cost are chosen: Typhoon destroys the Equip Spell first, the targeted Mystical Elf is never Summoned, and the 800 LP remain paid. This follows the historical distinction between an Equip Spell removed before its effect resolves and one destroyed after it has revived a monster. Imperial Order and Emergency Provisions interactions remain untested.

**Call of the Haunted:** [Historical Book of Moon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) say that turning a monster revived by Call face-down severs the link while the monster remains on the field and Call remains face-up meaninglessly. A seeded native fixture discards Mystical Elf with Graceful Charity, Sets Call, activates it on a later turn to revive the Elf, and activates Book of Moon. After resolution the Elf is face-down and still controlled by the Call player, while Call remains face-up. The opponent's current field view hides the Elf's identity but retains memory of its previously revealed identity. [Historical Mystical Space Typhoon rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html) distinguish Typhoon chained to Call from Call chained to Typhoon. Two native branches now cover both orders: when Typhoon is chained to Call, it destroys Call before Call resolves, so no Summon placement occurs and Mystical Elf remains in the Graveyard. When Call is chained to Typhoon, Call resolves first and the core requests a Summon zone; Typhoon then destroys Call and the revived Elf. Both branches end with the Elf in the Graveyard, so the fixture explicitly checks the interim Summon placement prompt and the different Graveyard event order.

**Airknight Parshath:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) require a draw whenever Airknight inflicts Battle Damage, including piercing damage against a Defense Position monster, but no draw if Waboku reduces that damage to zero. Paired seeded native fixtures discard Airknight with Graceful Charity, revive it with Premature Burial, and attack a set 7 Colored Fish (800 DEF). Without Waboku, the opponent loses 1100 LP and Airknight's controller draws one card. In the second branch, the opponent chains a set Waboku during the Battle Step; the Fish survives, no battle damage is dealt, and neither the hand nor Deck count changes from Airknight's effect. The attack-target action hides the set Fish's identity from the attacking player. A third branch attacks set Dekoichi (1000 DEF): Airknight inflicts 900 damage, its mandatory chain draws one card before Dekoichi’s Flip Effect chain draws for the defender, and both effects resolve during the Damage Step. Direct attacks and other damage modifiers remain untested.

**Jinzo:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings4.html) forbid Trap activation while Jinzo is face-up, but permit it once Jinzo is face-down. A seeded native fixture discards Jinzo with Graceful Charity, revives it with Premature Burial, and has the opponent Set Mirror Force. When face-up Jinzo declares a direct attack, Mirror Force is absent from the defender's legal chain actions; Jinzo deals 2400 damage and Mirror Force remains Set. In a paired branch, the defender Sets Book of Moon too and activates it on Jinzo before the next Main Phase. Jinzo stays face-down after Premature Burial falls away, the attacking player Normal Summons X-Head Cannon, and Mirror Force is then offered against X-Head Cannon's attack and destroys it. The attacker sees hidden backrow identities throughout. Continuous Trap effects already active before Jinzo is Summoned and summon-negation windows involving Jinzo remain untested.

**Magician of Faith:** A seeded native fixture activates Pot of Greed, Sets Magician of Faith, then Flip Summons it on the next turn and selects Pot from its Graveyard. Pot returns to hand and becomes a legal activation; the opponent receives the card reveal. In a paired branch, the opponent chains Book of Moon to the Flip Effect after Pot is targeted. Magician of Faith becomes face-down, but the Flip Effect still returns Pot. This follows the [historical Book of Moon ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) that a Flip Effect normally resolves after its monster is turned face-down. The opponent's field view hides the face-down card while retaining knowledge of the previously revealed Magician of Faith. Battle flips, Necrovalley, and recurring Flip Effect loops remain untested.

**Enemy Controller:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) distinguish its two modes and say that Tributing a monster for temporary control is an activation cost. A seeded native fixture Sets Enemy Controller while the opponent sees only a hidden backrow card, then offers both modes after the opponent Summons a face-up monster. The position mode turns that monster to Defense Position without Tributing the controller's own monster. The control mode Tributes the controller's monster before selecting the opposing monster, moves the target to the controller's field on resolution, and returns it to its original controller at the End Phase. Both modes load the pinned official script. Book of Moon changing the target face-down in response and control-return interactions with other effects remain untested.

**Exiled Force:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) say its self-Tribute is an activation cost, its Ignition Effect can use summon priority, and it cannot activate if it is the only monster on the field. A seeded native fixture has the opponent Set a monster, then Normal Summons Exiled Force and activates its effect. The cost places Exiled Force in its owner's Graveyard before target selection. The acting player's legal target action names only the opponent's field slot, without the hidden monster's identity; the effect destroys that monster on resolution. A second fixture verifies that no effect activation is offered with Exiled Force alone on the field. Responses to the Summon, chained target removal, and effect negation remain untested.

**Dust Tornado:** Its [official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4988&ope=2&request_locale=en) permits setting a Spell or Trap from hand after destroying the targeted opposing Spell or Trap. The [historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) require Dust Tornado itself to destroy that target before the optional Set is available. A paired seeded native fixture targets a hidden set Sakuretsu Armor by field slot, confirms its destruction, and checks both declining and accepting the Set choice. In the accepting branch, Book of Moon is the only Set candidate, remains hidden from the opponent, cannot activate during the controller's same-turn open Main Phase despite a face-up target, and becomes a legal chain response on the opponent's following turn. A third seeded branch targets one of two hidden opposing backrow cards, then the defender chains a previously Set Mystical Space Typhoon to destroy that target before Dust Tornado resolves. Dust Tornado offers no Set choice and the prospective Book of Moon remains in hand. Activating the targeted card without removing it, other Set card types, and chains involving Heavy Storm remain untested.

**Spirit Reaper:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html) say that a set Reaper flipped by battle survives destruction, that a face-up Reaper targeted by an effect normally destroys itself after that effect resolves, and that Book of Moon turning it face-down prevents that self-destruction. A seeded native fixture sets GOAT-variant Spirit Reaper (`504700038`), has a stronger monster attack it, and checks that it remains face-up in Defense Position with no battle damage. In one Main Phase 2 branch, Enemy Controller changes its position while leaving it face-up; Reaper destroys itself after resolution. In the other, Book of Moon sets Reaper, which survives and is hidden from the opponent's current field view while its previously public identity stays in known-card memory. A separate paired fixture follows the historical [Snatch Steal/Mystical Space Typhoon ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html): without Typhoon, Snatch targets face-up Reaper and it self-destructs; with Typhoon chained to destroy Snatch before Snatch resolves, Reaper remains face-up. Both fixtures confirm the dedicated GOAT script was loaded. A further paired fixture has Reaper attack directly into an opponent with six cards in hand or into a previously Set Waboku. Without Waboku, Reaper deals 300 damage and its mandatory effect discards one random opponent card into the public Graveyard; the acting player sees only the opponent hand count until that discard. With Waboku, no damage or discard occurs. Effect negation, an empty opponent hand, and targeting during the Damage Step remain untested.

**Morphing Jar:** [Official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4597&ope=2&request_locale=en) discards both players' entire hands, then makes each draw five cards. A seeded native fixture Sets Morphing Jar, Flip Summons it on the next turn, and compares both pre-Flip hands with the public Graveyards after resolution: each player's six cards were discarded, each Deck count fell by five, and each player has a new private five-card hand. A differential fixture changes one opponent Deck card that is drawn by Jar while leaving both pre-Flip hands and public discards identical. The acting player's post-resolution observation and legal actions remain identical across those runs, while the opponent's new private hand differs. The [historical deck-out ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html), chained Flip resets, and discard-trigger interactions remain untested.

**D.D. Warrior Lady versus Morphing Jar:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings2.html) place Morphing Jar's mandatory Flip Effect on Chain Link 1 and D.D. Warrior Lady's optional post-battle effect on Chain Link 2. A paired seeded native fixture attacks a hidden set Morphing Jar with D.D. Warrior Lady. After Jar flips face-up in the Damage Step, the attacker is offered the optional chain response. Declining lets Jar resolve its hand reset, then sends the battle-destroyed Jar to the Graveyard while Warrior Lady remains. Accepting banishes both monsters before Jar resolves, but Jar still discards both players' old hands and draws five new cards for each. The fixture checks public banished and Graveyard zones, Deck counts, and player-scoped hands in both branches. Other battle partners, effect negation, and chained Damage Step responses remain untested.

**Asura Priest versus Scapegoat:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings1.html) permit Asura Priest to attack each opposing monster once, forbid a direct attack after it has attacked a monster, and return a face-up Spirit monster to hand in the End Phase. A seeded native fixture has the defender activate GOAT-variant Scapegoat, then the attacker Normal Summons Asura Priest. The core offers a separate target choice before each of four attacks; each Sheep Token disappears without entering a Graveyard or banished zone. No direct attack is offered after the fourth Token, both Life Point totals remain 8000, and Asura Priest returns to its owner's hand in the End Phase. Surviving monsters that can be attacked only once, interrupted attacks, and a direct attack before any monster battle remain untested.

**Kycoo the Ghost Destroyer versus Chaos:** [Historical rulings](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings4.html) state that Kycoo prevents its opponent from removing cards from either Graveyard while it is face-up, but does not bar removal from the field, hand, or Deck. In a seeded native fixture, Chaos Sorcerer and the LIGHT/DARK monsters needed for its summon are available, yet no Special Summon is offered while the opposing Kycoo is face-up. Book of Moon turns Kycoo face-down; Sorcerer's Special Summon then becomes legal and succeeds after banishing the LIGHT and DARK monsters from its controller's Graveyard. A separate fixture has Kycoo deal 1800 direct battle damage after the opponent discards two monsters with Graceful Charity. Its optional effect offers either monster individually or both together as Graveyard banish targets; choosing both moves those two monsters to the banished zone, leaving Graceful Charity in the Graveyard. A third fixture confirms the historical exception: with opposing Kycoo still face-up, GOAT-variant Nobleman of Crossout remains legal and banishes its controller's set monster directly from the field. Kycoo's battle-damage effect against a monster, interrupted chains, and other non-Graveyard banish effects remain untested.

**Gravekeeper's Spy Deck search:** [The official card text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=5509&ope=2) permits Spy to Special Summon a Gravekeeper's monster with at most 1500 ATK from its controller's Deck. The GOAT whitelist uses variant `504700044` with a dedicated script. A seeded fixture Sets and Flip Summons Spy, then offers two copies of Gravekeeper's Guard and one further Spy from the Deck. Choosing Guard Special Summons it face-up, reduces the Deck count by one, and exposes the new monster to the opponent only after it reaches the field. A paired deck-order differential changes a native Deck slot without changing the player-visible observation or legal actions. Adapter actions now omit Deck and Extra Deck slot numbers and order ordinary Deck choices by visible card data. A second seeded fixture follows the historical [Necrovalley ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings3.html): with pre-errata Necrovalley active, Spy is still an eligible Deck target based on its original 1200 ATK, and the summoned Spy has 1700 ATK and 2500 DEF after the field bonus. For an empty search, the dedicated GOAT Spy script calls a shared Deck-confirm helper. The pinned helper originally addressed the searching player; GOAT AI changes that recipient at runtime to the opponent, following [GoatFormat’s verification rule](https://www.goatformat.com/basics.html) and the analogous [Sangan no-target ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings7.html). A one-Spy fixture confirms a 33-card Deck reveal only in the opponent’s view, no Special Summon, and a distinct hash for the loaded utility script. A direct historical Spy-specific verification source has not been located, so this recipient choice remains a documented interpretation. Interrupted Flip Effects remain untested.

**Mystic Tomato battle recruiter:** [The official text](https://www.db.yugioh-card.com/yugiohdb/card_search.action?cid=4930&ope=2) requires battle destruction and a trip to the Graveyard, then permits a face-up Attack Position summon of a DARK monster with at most 1500 ATK from the Deck. A seeded native fixture has Gemini Elf attack a set GOAT-variant Tomato. The attacker chooses only a hidden field slot. After Tomato reaches the Graveyard in the Damage Step, the defender may decline the optional effect or accept and choose from two remaining Tomatoes and GOAT-variant Sangan, with no Deck slot numbers exposed. Choosing Sangan Special Summons it in face-up Attack Position without changing Life Points; declining leaves the Deck count unchanged. A second fixture has Tomato attack a stronger Gemini Elf on its controller's turn, take 500 battle damage, and recruit Sangan. The core then offers Sangan an attack before Main Phase 2, matching the [historical ruling](https://www.goatformat.com/rulings5.html). Control-change, battle redirection, effect negation, and no-target verification remain untested.
